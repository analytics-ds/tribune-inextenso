## Key Takeaways

1. Cash flow is immediate liquidity: cash on hand, bank accounts, very short-term investments
2. Three management pillars: forecast flows (predictions), control them (inflows and outflows), optimize them (placements)
3. Common mistakes: ignoring payment delays, failing to anticipate seasonality, confusing profit with cash flow
4. Essential tools: monthly dashboards, quarterly forecasts, updated accounting software

## Why Cash Flow Matters

Unlike accounting profit which measures profitability, cash flow measures the ability to pay debts immediately. A company can be profitable and go bankrupt from lack of liquidity if it doesn't properly manage payment delays and flows.

For SME leaders, this distinction is critical: a customer paying at 60 days paralyzes your cash flow, even if the contract is profitable.

### Three Pillars of Cash Management

**1. Forecast** : anticipate inflows and outflows over 13 weeks minimum
**2. Control** : ensure collections match the planned schedule
**3. Optimize** : place surpluses, negotiate payment terms with suppliers and customers

## Cash Flow Management Basics

### The Cash Budget

Unlike the operating budget which measures expenses, the cash budget records actual money movements: collected VAT, paid VAT, customer deposits, net salaries after deductions.

A 1,000 euro invoice recorded in results is only impactful to cash flow upon collection. If the customer pays in 60 days, you wait 60 days before touching the money.

### The Cash Conversion Cycle

This is the delay between paying suppliers and collecting from customers. For a typical trading company:

- Supplier terms: 30 days
- Stock: 15 days average sale duration
- Customer terms: 45 days

Your cash cycle = (15 + 45) - 30 = 30 days. During this period, sales money is "blocked" financing operations.

### Key Indicators

Track these three figures for 95% of operational decisions:

| Indicator | Formula | Frequency |
|-----------|---------|-----------|
| Minimum expected balance | Min(forecast flows) | Weekly |
| Days of cash | Current balance ÷ daily expenses | Weekly |
| Financing need | Cash cycle × CA/365 | Monthly |

## Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Confusing profit and cash flow** : customers owe you money, but you don't have cash in hand yet
2. **Ignoring seasonality** : seasonal business creates cash peaks and troughs, plan ahead
3. **Neglecting payment terms** : 30 additional days costs 2-3 months of operational financing
4. **No backup plan** : a short-term credit line (authorized overdraft) is essential insurance

## Conclusion

Managing cash flow is taking control of your business's financial oxygen. Tools abound (simple Excel, integrated software, or specialized advice), but the intention is key: anticipate and control, rather than suffer.

For leaders asking where to start: a simple 13-week cash forecast, updated every Friday, solves 80% of liquidity crises.
