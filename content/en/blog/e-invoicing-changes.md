## Key Takeaways

1. E-invoicing mandatory since July 2024 (SMEs 10+) and for microenterprises by July 2025
2. Required format: e-FACTUR or UBL, sent via Chorus Pro or certified software
3. Common mistakes: PDF format non-compliant, missing mandatory mentions, incomplete data
4. Solutions: adopt certified invoicing software and train the team

## Mandatory Timeline

| Category | Deadline |
|----------|----------|
| SMEs (10+ employees) | July 1, 2024 (already passed) |
| Large enterprises | January 1, 2024 (already passed) |
| Microenterprises (0-9) | July 1, 2025 |
| Self-employed | July 1, 2025 |

If you're an SME, you're already behind if you haven't switched. You have a few months to regularize.

## What Makes a Compliant E-Invoice?

An e-invoice isn't just a PDF. It's a structured file (XML) containing mandatory elements and specific formats accepted by tax authorities.

## Solutions for Compliance

### Option 1: Chorus Pro

Public platform for invoicing public administration clients.

### Option 2: Certified Invoicing Software

Invoice private and public clients using your business software.

Examples: Sage 100, Tiime, Exact (many now offer this).

### Option 3: API from Certified Provider

Integrate compliant invoice generation into your own application.

## Conclusion

E-invoicing is not optional: it's a legal requirement. Beyond compliance, it's an opportunity to simplify processes and improve management.

Contact your accountant to validate your solution and ensure compliant archiving.
